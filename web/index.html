    About this map

    The 213,000 buildings of inner London: the City, Westminster, Camden, Islington, Kensington and Chelsea, Southwark, Lambeth, Tower Hamlets and Hackney. Each is coloured by the year it was built where a source knows it, and set in the history it belongs to.

    A non-commercial project, made out of curiosity and for education.

    Made by Alex Barakov.

    Inspired by Sasha Bogachev and his Paris, building by building.

    LAYRS projects

    LAYRS maps cities building by building: each one with its age and the layers of history it belongs to.

    • LAYRS: London, you are here.
    • LAYRS: Amsterdam: 192,000 buildings of Amsterdam, from the canal ring to the raid on Noord.
    • LAYRS: Delft: 37,000 buildings of Delft, from Vermeer's street to the 1654 gunpowder blast.
    • LAYRS: St. Petersburg: 107,000 buildings of St. Petersburg (in Russian).

    Open source

    The code and the pipeline are on GitHub. Know a piece of London's history that lives in its buildings? Add it as a layer. Know when a building was built? Add it to Colouring London, where this map takes its years from.

    Data

    Downloaded in October 2026.

    • Footprints: Overture Maps, from OpenStreetMap contributors and Microsoft. ODbL.
    • Heights, and the towers, spires and domes above them: Environment Agency LIDAR composite, 1 m. Open Government Licence.
    • Years, most precise first: Colouring London contributors (ODbL); National Heritage List for England descriptions, Historic England (OGL), read from British Listed Buildings; Wikidata (CC0) and OpenStreetMap (ODbL); London Development Database completions, GLA (OGL); HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, new-build sales, with the ONS UPRN Directory (OGL); Lambeth and Southwark local lists (OGL); construction age bands of the GLA London Building Stock Model 2, from energy certificates (OGL); the Ordnance Survey map of 1891–96 as vectorised in A Layer of Late Victorian London (Petitpierre, McDonough, Fleet and others, CC BY 4.0) and the Ordnance Survey 1:1250 plans of the 1940s–70s, National Library of Scotland (CC BY).
    • Listing: Historic England, via Colouring London.
    • Borough boundaries: Office for National Statistics. Open Government Licence.
    • Names, photos, stories: Wikidata, Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons; each photo credits its author and licence.
    • Street names and water: OpenStreetMap contributors. ODbL.

    Also as plain pages, for reading and for search engines: London, building by building.

    Method

    London has no open register of construction years, so each building takes its date from the most precise open source that has one, and the card names it. An exact year is drawn in full colour; a range (a century in a listing, an energy-certificate band, “after 1952” because the building is not yet on that year's map) is drawn paler and can be hidden with “Exact years only”. Nothing is borrowed from neighbours and no model estimates are used: buildings no source dates stay grey. Heights are the 70th percentile of the LIDAR surface over the ground around each footprint. Every hand-written story is checked against its Wikipedia article.
