Every building standing today, by age and historical context.

About this map

The 213,000 buildings of inner London: the City, Westminster, Camden, Islington, Kensington and Chelsea, Southwark, Lambeth, Tower Hamlets and Hackney. Each is coloured by the year it was built where a source knows it, and set in the history it belongs to.

A non-commercial project, made out of curiosity and for education.

Made by Alex Barakov.

Inspired by Sasha Bogachev and his Paris, building by building.

LAYRS projects

LAYRS maps cities building by building: each one with its age and the layers of history it belongs to.

  • LAYRS: London, you are here.
  • LAYRS: Amsterdam: 192,000 buildings of Amsterdam, from the canal ring to the raid on Noord.
  • LAYRS: Delft: 37,000 buildings of Delft, from Vermeer's street to the 1654 gunpowder blast.
  • LAYRS: St. Petersburg: 107,000 buildings of St. Petersburg (in Russian).

Open source

The code and the pipeline are on GitHub. Know a piece of London's history that lives in its buildings? Add it as a layer. Know when a building was built? Add it to Colouring London, where this map takes its years from.

Data

Downloaded in October 2026.

  • Footprints: Overture Maps, from OpenStreetMap contributors and Microsoft. ODbL.
  • Heights: Environment Agency LIDAR composite, 1 m. Open Government Licence.
  • Years and listing: Colouring London (Colouring Cities Research Programme) and its contributors; listing from Historic England. ODbL.
  • Borough boundaries: Office for National Statistics. Open Government Licence.
  • The 1890s map: A Layer of Late Victorian London by Petitpierre, McDonough, Fleet and others, footprints from the Ordnance Survey 1:1,056 map of 1891–1896. CC BY 4.0.
  • Names, photos, stories: Wikidata, Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons; each photo credits its author and licence.
  • Street names and water: OpenStreetMap contributors. ODbL.

Method

London has no open register of construction years, so a building shows a year only where Colouring London's contributors recorded one, most often by comparing historical maps; years with a range are paler and marked “c.”. The rest, about three in four, are grey and appear only in today's city. Heights are the 70th percentile of the LIDAR surface over the ground around each footprint. Every hand-written story is checked against its Wikipedia article.
